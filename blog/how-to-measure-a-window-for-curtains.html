How to measure a window for curtains: A complete step-by-step guide

Curtains can transform the appearance, privacy, lighting, and comfort of any room. However, even premium curtains may look poorly fitted when the width, length, rod position, or fabric fullness is measured incorrectly. Curtains that are too narrow can leave visible gaps, while curtains that are too short may make an otherwise elegant interior appear unfinished.

Learning how to measure window for curtains helps you select the right curtain size, rod width, track position, heading style, and finishing length. Whether you are ordering ready-made curtains or investing in custom luxury curtains, accurate measurements are essential. This complete guide from Curtivelle explains the process in 10 clear steps so you can achieve a balanced and professionally fitted result.

10 clear steps to measure a window for curtains

Proper window measurement helps you get curtains that fit beautifully, hang smoothly, and cover the window correctly. Before ordering custom curtains, measure both width and height carefully so the final look feels balanced, elegant, and professional. Here is a general step-by-step process given below:

Step 1: Prepare the right measuring tools

Accurate curtain measurement begins with suitable tools. Using the wrong type of measuring tape or recording approximate numbers may result in poor-fitting curtains. A metal measuring tape is more reliable than a flexible sewing tape because it remains straight and does not stretch. Choose either centimeters or inches and use the same unit for every measurement.

Measure each area at least twice before recording the final number. For large windows, ask another person to hold the tape so it remains straight and level. You will need:

A strong metal measuring tape

A pencil or pen

A notebook or measurement sheet

A step ladder for tall windows

A spirit level, if available

A mobile phone for reference photos

Another person to assist with large windows

Step 2: Choose the curtain installation position

Before measuring the width or height, decide where the curtain rod or track will be installed. The installation position affects the curtain size and the visual proportion of the room.

A standard curtain rod is generally installed around 10 to 15 centimeters above the window frame. Hanging the rod closer to the ceiling can make the window and room appear taller.

The rod should also extend beyond both sides of the window. An extension of around 15 to 25 centimeters on each side usually allows the curtains to open fully without covering too much glass. Consider these installation options:

Above-window wall-mounted rod

Wall-mounted curtain track

Ceiling-mounted track

Wall-to-wall curtain system

Inside-frame curtain fitting

Bay-window track or connected rod

Step 3: Measure the window and curtain rod width

Measure the complete width of the window from one outer edge of the frame to the other. Do not measure only the visible glass area unless the curtains will be installed inside the frame. If the measurements differ slightly, use the largest one to ensure complete coverage.

When the rod is already installed, measure only the usable rod width. Do not include decorative finials because curtains do not hang over them.

When the rod has not yet been installed, calculate the required width by adding extension space to both sides of the window.

For example:
Window width: 150 cm
Left-side extension: 20 cm
Right-side extension: 20 cm
Recommended rod width: 190 cm

Heavy curtains may require more side space because thick fabric occupies a larger area when stacked open. Take width measurements at three positions:

Across the top

Across the middle

Across the bottom

Step 4: Calculate the correct curtain fullness

Curtain fullness means the amount of fabric used in relation to the curtain rod or track width. Proper fullness creates soft folds and prevents curtains from looking flat or stretched.

For example, if the rod is 200 centimeters wide and you require two times fullness:

200 cm × 2 = 400 cm total curtain width

For a pair of curtains, each panel should be approximately 200 centimeters wide.

The ideal fullness depends on the curtain fabric, heading style, room size, and desired visual effect. Sheer curtains usually need more fabric to create elegant gathering, while thick velvet curtains may require less. Common curtain fullness ratios include:

1.5 times fullness: Suitable for heavy and thick fabrics

2 times fullness: Recommended for most residential curtains

2.5 times fullness: Suitable for sheers and luxury curtains

3 times fullness: Ideal for highly gathered decorative curtains

Step 5: Select the curtain length style

Before measuring the curtain height, decide where the curtains should finish. Different curtain lengths suit different windows, rooms, and interior styles. Sill-length curtains are practical for kitchens, bathrooms, and windows above counters. Below-sill curtains provide additional coverage without reaching the floor.

Floor-length curtains create a clean and modern appearance and are ideal for bedrooms, living rooms, dining rooms, hotels, and offices. Trouser-break curtains extend slightly onto the floor, while puddle curtains use additional fabric to create a dramatic and luxurious finish.

Choose the final curtain style before taking the height measurement, as each option requires a different finishing point.

The main curtain length options are the following:

Window sill length

Below-sill length

Floor length

Trouser-break length

Puddle length

Step 6: Measure the curtain length correctly

Curtain length should be measured from the rod, ring, hook, or track to the selected finishing point. The correct starting position depends on the curtain heading style. Floors and ceilings may not be completely level. Multiple measurements help prevent one side of the curtain from appearing shorter than the other.

For a modern and neat appearance, floor-length curtains should stop just above the floor or lightly touch it. Use these measurement guidelines:

Sill-length curtains: Measure to around one centimeter above the sill.

Below-sill curtains: Measure to around 10 to 15 centimeters below the sill.

Floor-length curtains: Measure one or two centimeters above the floor.

Trouser-break curtains: Measure to the floor and add around 3 to 5 centimeters.

Puddle curtains: Measure to the floor and add around 15 to 30 centimeters.

Note: Measure the height at three points:

Left side

Centre

Right side

Step 7: Account for the curtain heading style

The curtain heading is the upper section that connects the fabric to the rod or track. Different heading styles hang from different positions, so the measurement starting point must be adjusted accordingly. Common curtain heading styles include:

For eyelet curtains, measure from the top of the rod to the desired finishing point. Remember that a small portion of fabric usually sits above the eyelet.

For pencil-pleat and pinch-pleat curtains, measure from the bottom of the ring or the hook position.

For wave curtains and ceiling-mounted tracks, measure from the bottom of the track to the floor.

For tab-top curtains, measure from the top of the rod, considering the visible height of the fabric tabs.

Step 8: Check furniture, fixtures, and window obstacles

Curtains need enough space to hang and move freely. Before finalizing measurements, inspect the entire area around the window. A window handle may prevent a blind or inside-frame curtain from closing properly. A radiator below the window may also make floor-length curtains unsuitable.

When furniture is placed directly below the window, sill-length or below-sill curtains may be more practical. However, you may still choose floor-length curtains if the furniture can be repositioned.

Also confirm that the curtains will not block vents, switches, or frequently opened windows.

Check for:

Beds and sofas

Desks and cabinets

Kitchen counters

Radiators

Air-conditioning units

Window handles

Electrical switches

Wall lights

Shelves

Ceiling beams

Decorative wall features

Step 9: Measure special windows and curtain tracks

Some window styles require additional measurements and planning. Bay windows, wide glass walls, corner windows, and ceiling tracks are more complex than standard rectangular windows.

For wall-to-wall windows, measure the entire wall width and take ceiling-to-floor measurements at several points.

Complex window shapes often benefit from professional measurement because incorrect angles or track lengths can affect curtain movement and alignment. For curtain tracks:

Measure the full required track width

Add enough space beyond the window edges

Measure the track-to-floor height

Check whether the track is wall-mounted or ceiling-mounted

Confirm whether the track will be straight or curved

For bay windows:

Measure each straight section separately

Record the angles between sections

Measure the bay depth

Check window handles and opening directions

Note built-in seating, radiators, and shelves

Decide whether to use separate rods or a flexible track

Step 10: Record, label, and double-check everything

Do not assume that two similar-looking windows have identical dimensions. Small construction differences can affect the final curtain fit. Label every window clearly, such as: Living Room Window 1, Living Room Window 2, Dining room window 1, Master Bedroom Left Window, Office Front Window.

Take clear photos of the window and surrounding wall. These photos can help your curtain consultant understand the available installation space.

Avoid rounding measurements unnecessarily. Accurate figures provide a better fit, especially for custom-made luxury curtains.

Before ordering, double-check that:

The same unit has been used throughout

Decorative finials were excluded

Side extensions were included

Fullness was calculated correctly

The heading style was selected

Height was measured at three points

Nearby furniture was considered

Each window was measured separately

Common curtain-measuring mistakes to avoid

Even a small measuring error can affect curtain coverage, appearance, and installation. Review the measurements carefully before placing an order. Professional measurement is recommended when dealing with bay windows, very tall windows, ceiling tracks, large glass walls, or custom luxury curtains.

Common mistakes include:

Measuring only the glass area

Forgetting curtain fullness

Using a flexible fabric tape

Taking only one height measurement

Including decorative finials in rod width

Ignoring uneven floors and ceilings

Ordering before choosing a heading style

Forgetting curtain stacking space

Mixing centimetres and inches

Ignoring nearby furniture and fixtures

Assuming all windows are identical

Using estimated instead of exact measurements
