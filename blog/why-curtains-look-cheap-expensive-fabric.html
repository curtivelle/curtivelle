Why your curtains look cheap even when the fabric is expensive

You picked a beautiful fabric. You paid a premium price for it. And yet, once the curtains are hanging in your living room, something feels off. They look flat, a little stiff, and almost "off-the-rack," even though you know for a fact that the material itself is high quality. If this sounds familiar, you're not alone, and more importantly, it's not your fabric's fault.

At Curtivelle, we've dressed windows across Gulshan, Banani, Baridhara, and beyond. We can tell you this with confidence: expensive fabric is only one ingredient in a luxury curtain. The rest comes down to construction, proportion, and finishing and the details most ready-made curtain sellers skip entirely. In this blog we will get a clear understanding about this issue.

Reasons for expensive curtains that look cheap

Here's exactly why your curtains might be underperforming and what separates a curtain that looks "fine" from one that looks genuinely luxurious.

1. Not enough fullness in the fabric

This is, by far, the number one reason curtains look cheap. Fullness refers to how much fabric is used relative to the width of your window or curtain rod.

Most mass-market or budget-tailored curtains use a fullness ratio of around 1.5x—meaning the fabric is only one-and-a-half times the width of the rod. This makes curtains hang flat, with wide, sparse folds that look thin no matter how nice the fabric is.

Luxury curtains typically use a 2x to 2.5x fullness ratio. That means significantly more fabric bunched into soft, consistent folds. This single change — using more fabric, not different fabric — is often what makes the biggest visual difference in a room.

The fix: Always calculate fullness before cutting. For heavier fabrics like velvet, 2x is usually enough. For sheer or lightweight linen, aim closer to 2.5x–3x for that soft, cloud-like drape.

2. Skipping (or skimping on) interlining and lining

A gorgeous face fabric with no proper lining behind it is like a beautiful outfit with no structure underneath—it just doesn't hold its shape. Lined and interlined curtains (a soft flannel-like layer sandwiched between the face fabric and the lining) add weight, body, and that heavy, expensive drape you see in five-star hotels and high-end homes.

Unlined curtains:

  • Let sunlight fade and weaken the fabric faster
  • Hang limp instead of falling in structured folds
  • Show every seam, shadow, and imperfection from outside

The fix: Never leave statement fabrics—silk, velvet, or jacquard—unlined. A proper cotton or blackout lining, plus interlining for heavier drapes, instantly upgrades the finished look and also improves insulation and light control, which matters a lot in Dhaka's climate.

3. Poor hemming and uneven weights

Cheap-looking curtains often have hems that are too shallow, uneven, or entirely machine-finished without any weight inside them. This causes curtains to flip, curl at the edges, or hang unevenly—especially near windows with airflow or ceiling fans.

High-end curtain makers use:

  • Deep hems (often 4–6 inches at the bottom)
  • Hand-stitched blind hems so no stitching is visible from the front
  • Small weights sewn into the bottom corners to help the fabric hang straight and resist curling

The fix: Insist on generous, weighted hems rather than the thin, quick hems used in factory-made curtains. It's a small detail that makes a huge difference in how the curtain behaves over time.

4. The wrong curtain length

Even the most expensive fabric looks wrong if the length is off. Curtains that stop a few inches above the floor, or that awkwardly skim it, immediately read as budget or mismeasured.

There are really only two lengths that look intentional and high-end:

  • Just grazing the floor—a clean, tailored look
  • A gentle break or puddle—where fabric softly pools on the floor for a more dramatic, luxurious effect

The fix: Always measure from the exact rod or track height in the room after the hardware is installed—not from a generic "standard length" assumption.

5. Mounting the rod too low or too narrow

This one has nothing to do with the fabric at all, and yet it affects the entire perceived quality of the curtain.

If your curtain rod is mounted just above the window frame and barely wider than the window itself, the curtains will look cramped and the ceiling will feel lower than it is.

The fix: Mount the rod higher — ideally close to the ceiling or crown molding — and extend it several inches beyond the window frame on each side. This makes the window appear larger, lets in more light when curtains are open, and gives the fabric room to fall in fuller, more elegant folds.

6. Mismatched curtains and interior style

Sometimes the fabric itself is genuinely expensive, but the style, print, or color simply clashes with the room. A heavy brocade in a minimalist space, or a stiff, formal fabric in a soft, contemporary room, can feel "off" even if it's technically high-quality.

The fix: Choose fabric weight, texture, and pattern based on the room's overall style—not just the price tag or the fabric on its own. A good curtain consultant will guide fabric choice based on your existing furniture, wall color, and natural light, not just what looks nice on a swatch card.

7. Rushed or inconsistent stitching

Finally, look closely at the seams, pleats, and header. Uneven pleat spacing, visible puckering, or inconsistent header pinch (especially in pinch-pleat or pencil-pleat styles) will make even the best fabric look poorly made.

The fix: This is where craftsmanship matters more than material. Well-spaced, symmetrical pleats and a clean, structured header are what give curtains that polished, tailored appearance—the kind you notice in high-end interiors without quite knowing why it looks so good.

The real formula for luxury curtains

Luxury curtains are not defined by expensive fabric alone. The final look depends on how the fabric is measured, tailored, lined, and installed. When every detail is handled properly, even a simple fabric can look refined and premium.

  • Quality fabric: Choose a fabric that suits your interior style, lighting, and desired level of luxury.
  • Correct fullness: The right fullness creates a richer, more elegant drape instead of a flat appearance.
  • Proper lining: Good lining improves the curtain’s fall, privacy, light control, and durability.
  • Accurate length and placement: Precise measurements and well-positioned hardware help curtains frame the window beautifully.
  • Skilled stitching and finishing: Professional tailoring ensures clean seams, balanced hems, and a polished final look.
